Studying how everyday chemical exposures shape children's growth and development.

I'm a doctoral researcher in the Nutrition and Food Science department at the University of Granada, investigating how endocrine-disrupting chemicals such as bisphenols and parabens — found widely in food packaging and everyday products — interact with genetics and gut microbiota to influence childhood obesity and neurodevelopment. My work combines biomonitoring, dietary exposure modelling and cognitive-behavioural testing in Spanish school-age populations.

Research focus

My PhD thesis, directed by Prof. Ana María Rivas Velasco and Prof. Alberto Zafra Gómez, examines the effect of endocrine-disrupting compound exposure on neurodevelopment in children. The central hypothesis: genetic variability and changes in gut microbiota may explain why exposure to the same chemicals affects some children's cognitive and metabolic health more than others.

In practice, this means recruiting cohorts of Spanish schoolchildren, measuring bisphenols and parabens in urine, hair and nail samples via UHPLC-MS/MS, and pairing those measurements with validated dietary questionnaires (24h recalls, FFQs), genotyping panels, and standardised cognitive-behavioural instruments (WISC-V, BASC-3).

Core research threads

  • Dietary & biomonitoring exposure assessment to bisphenols and parabens
  • Gene × chemical-exposure interactions in childhood obesity
  • Endocrine disruptors and cognitive / behavioural outcomes
  • Gut microbiota as a mediator of obesity–neurodevelopment links
